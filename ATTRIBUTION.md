# Data attribution

*異世界で日本語しか使えません！* is an original learning story written for this project from the user's premise. Its prose is not copied from the vocabulary workbook, grammar PDF, JMdict examples, or another novel. Target IDs refer to the user's N3 collection; reader-only corrections and contextual grammar annotations are documented in `README.md` and the coverage report. The practice collections are preserved.

The supporting reader dictionary selects additional glosses/readings from the same JMdict English XML used for the study collections and supplements them with existing N2 translations and editorial definitions for names, particles and common words. `src/data/novel-lexicon.json` marks each entry's source. JMdict-derived glosses retain the CC BY-SA 4.0 attribution and licence described below, also linked in the reader. Vietnamese translations/contextual glosses for the 831 additional JMdict entries are editorially supplied in `scripts/novel-lexicon-vi.tsv` and bundled alongside the existing translations; the same attribution and licence apply to these JMdict-derived translations. All supporting entries now include Vietnamese meanings. Additional Hán Việt character readings are selected from Unicode Unihan 17.0.0 `kVietnamese` and the existing study collections. They are Sino-Vietnamese character readings, not Japanese pronunciation or a semantic translation.

[Kuromoji.js](https://github.com/takuyaa/kuromoji.js) (Apache License 2.0) is used only as a build-time tokenizer to identify manuscript word forms. Its bundled IPADIC dictionary and tokenizer are not redistributed in the reader assets; runtime annotations use the small selected dictionary and reviewed inflection rules. No story text is sent to dictionary or translation services.

The N3 Hán Việt importer uses Unicode Unihan `kVietnamese` data and falls back to the [kanji-data-hanviet](https://github.com/NVL4826/kanji-data-hanviet) project for characters without a Unihan reading. The latter project is distributed under the MIT License (copyright 2019 David Gouveia). Its license is available at the linked repository.

The N3 workbook and N2 PDF were supplied by the user and are used as vocabulary sources for this local application.

The N3 grammar collection uses the user-provided **Ebook n3.pdf**, titled *Ngữ pháp tiếng Nhật N3 — Trọn bộ*, branded Riki nihongo. Its 111 numbered lessons occupy PDF pages 4–31; promotional pages are excluded. All 134 Japanese examples, Vietnamese translations, source formation notation and available furigana were transcribed with local OCR and visually reviewed. The bundled data retains source page references. English meanings and example translations are editorial additions, not JMdict data; Vietnamese meaning headings missing from lessons 15 and 16 are supplied from their examples. Lesson 4's first example lacks a Vietnamese translation, which is supplied editorially.

Obvious Japanese source typos in lessons 4, 31, 36, 67, 84, 85, 86, 88, 93 and 106 are corrected for study while preserving the original sentence in `sourceJapanese`, visible under the original-source disclosure. Lesson 13's mistyped pattern heading is normalized and noted. Other source wording and notation is retained, including the Japanese/Vietnamese tense discrepancy in lesson 69, which is noted. These source materials are not represented as licensed under JMdict's CC BY-SA terms. No source PDF or marketing images are included in the app.

The reproducible reviewed transcription is `scripts/n3_grammar_reviewed.txt`; `scripts/import_n3_grammar.py` adds transparent corrections and notes and produces `src/data/n3-grammar.json`. `scripts/ocr_n3_grammar.mjs` generates scratch OCR for a new visual review; it never replaces the reviewed text automatically. Studying sends no requests to Riki or translation services.

The 214-radical collection uses the user-supplied `214_Bo_Thu_Kanji_So_Tay_Hoc_Tap.pdf` for traditional ordering, glyphs, stroke counts, Vietnamese meanings and variants. Japanese radical names and English meanings are supplemented from [Kanji alive's Japanese radicals CSV](https://github.com/kanjialive/kanji-data-media/blob/master/language-data/japanese-radicals.csv), by the Kanji alive team, under [Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The CSV's radical encodings are mapped to Kangxi numbers, English glosses split into accepted answers, and Japanese names merged with the handbook. Traditional Sino-Vietnamese radical names are maintained in `scripts/import_radicals.py`. Source data is bundled in `src/data/radicals.json`; no reference-service requests occur while studying.

The English meanings use the English component of [JMdict](https://www.edrdg.org/wiki/index.php/JMdict-EDICT_Dictionary_Project), copyright James William BREEN and the Electronic Dictionary Research and Development Group. These glosses have been shortened, selected for the study lists, and supplemented with editorial overrides. The JMdict-derived English meanings are distributed under [Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). See [EDRDG's licence statement](https://www.edrdg.org/edrdg/licence.html) and the bundled [licence text](public/JMdict-LICENSE.txt).

To refresh the English meanings, download and decompress the current [JMdict English XML](https://www.edrdg.org/pub/Nihongo/JMdict_e.gz), then run `python scripts/import_english_meanings.py path/to/JMdict_e.xml`. This step must also be run after rebuilding either collection from the original Vietnamese sources. Reviewed translations and disambiguation are kept in `scripts/english_meanings_overrides.json`.
## Novel audio

The original novel's Japanese narration was generated using `ja-JP-KeitaNeural` through the build-only [edge-tts project](https://github.com/rany2/edge-tts) and Microsoft Edge's online speech service. These recordings narrate the original story, not the source vocabulary book or dictionary explanations. Word-boundary metadata from the synthesis service supplies sentence synchronization. [imageio-ffmpeg](https://github.com/imageio/imageio-ffmpeg) supplies FFmpeg for encoding chapter and complete-novel MP3 files. Neither Python package is shipped as frontend code. See `scripts/requirements-audio.txt` and `README.md` for the reproducible generation workflow.
